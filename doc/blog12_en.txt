    <div class="post-section">
        <h3>Preface</h3>

        <p>This interview is part of a collaboration between the <i>NYCU ESG Sustainable Prospect Club</i> and the <i>NTU Climate Action Club</i> for the project Climate Professionals. The theme centers on <strong>youth action</strong>—whether university students engaged in sustainability initiatives on campus, or recent graduates taking their first steps into the workforce. They are at a stage where exploration and practice go hand in hand.</p>

        <p>Through in-depth conversations with these young changemakers, we hope to spark new ideas and opportunities for action. This interview features <strong>Chia-Hsuan Chen</strong>, a student from National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University’s Arete Honors Program, majoring in Electrical Engineering. In 2022, she founded the <i>ESG Sustainable Prospect Club</i>, later participated in entrepreneurship competitions, and by 2025, joined a Silicon Valley accelerator, embarking on an international startup journey.</p>

    </div>

    <div class="post-section">
        <h3>If No One Is Doing It, It’s Not Because No One Has Thought About It—It’s Because It’s Really Hard</h3>

        <p>“In Taiwan, if no one says ‘yes,’ the answer is often assumed to be ‘no.’ But I like to ask: Why not?” With this mindset, Chia-Hsuan began a university journey that was both exhausting and full of challenges.</p>

        <p><blockquote>“I’ve always enjoyed solving problems, especially those related to sustainability,”</blockquote></p>
        <p>she recalls. In high school, she worked on a research project about automated water droplet movement—her first attempt to combine engineering with nature.</p>

        <p>At university, she encountered a broader range of sustainability issues and realized that while these problems are challenging, they also urgently need practical solutions. That’s when she decided to establish the <i>ESG Sustainable Prospect Club</i>.</p>

        <p>In 2022, as part of Taiwan’s <i>International Youth Ambassador</i> program, she visited Palau. There, through exchanges with local sustainability organizations, cultural groups, and even the president, she came face-to-face with the impacts of rising sea levels for the first time. She recalls vividly: “We drove across a bridge—though it was called a bridge, it stood only 50 centimeters to 1 meter above sea level, about waist to chest height for a person. What we often hear about sea-level rise as a distant concept was right there before my eyes. The island was slowly sinking…”</p>

        <p>When she returned to Taiwan, she realized that universities like NTU and NTHU had sustainability clubs, but NYCU did not. From the question “Why not?”, the <i>ESG Sustainable Prospect Club</i> was born.</p>

        <p><blockquote>“If we simply named it a ‘sustainability club,’ it would have been too vague. I wanted students to understand that ESG is not just a slogan—it’s a framework for seeing how sustainability connects with business, technology, and governance.”</blockquote></p>
    
        <p>As the club grew, she categorized its members into <strong>explorers</strong>, <strong>competitors</strong>, and <strong>career seekers</strong>. Most students fell into the third group, looking to leverage their experiences in sustainability for future career opportunities.</p>
    
    </div>

    <div class="post-section">
        <h3>If Someone Encourages You to Start a Business, Ask Them: Do You Have a Grudge Against Me?</h3>

        <p>Chia-Hsuan admits that the process of entrepreneurship is far more difficult than most imagine. Beyond technical challenges, there are administrative hurdles, patents, business models, and team management. Still, she chose this path—because it was a way to bring sustainability ideas into society. In one entrepreneurship competition, her team proposed the concept of a “<strong>Sponge Island</strong>”, inspired by Taiwan’s worst drought in half a century in 2021.</p>

        <p>“At the time, the government cut irrigation for 70% of farmland to divert water to industrial use. It even became international news. Since my father’s family is from Lukang, the issue felt very close to me.”</p>
    
        <p>The “ContiHydro” project led them to build an experimental underground reservoir on the NYCU campus. It even attracted the interest of the Taiwan Water Corporation. Yet, regulatory restrictions and business model challenges ultimately halted the project after two years. Even so, she views the experience as invaluable:</p>
    
        <p>“It was a precious lesson in entrepreneurship, even if the project didn’t last."</p>
    </div>

    <div class="post-section">
        <h3>Leadership Is Moving Forward Together</h3>
        
        <p>When it comes to leadership, Chia-Hsuan emphasizes the importance of her teammates. Reflecting on her time leading the <i>NYCU ESG Sustainable Prospect Club</i> and entrepreneurship projects, she notes that leadership involved more than just organizing activities—it also required managing administration, communication, and time.</p>

        <p><blockquote>“These challenges taught me that running a club can’t rely solely on passion. It requires endurance and the support of a team.”</blockquote></p>

        <p>She leaves this advice for future student leaders:</p>

        <p><blockquote>“Think of managing a club as a long-term experiment. There will be setbacks, but you need to adjust your mindset and turn every challenge into nourishment for growth.”</blockquote></p>
    </div>
